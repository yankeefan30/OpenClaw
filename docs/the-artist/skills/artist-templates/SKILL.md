# Narrative prompt packs

Fill brackets from the user request. Then run the normal pipeline on the CVS template.

## Template A — Board decision deck

```text
Create a board-ready decision deck.

Objective:
[OBJECTIVE]

Decision requested:
[DECISION]

Audience:
[BOARD AUDIENCE]

Business context:
[CONTEXT]

Source material:
[SOURCES]

Required slide count:
[SLIDE COUNT]

Required output:
1. Governing thought.
2. Executive summary.
3. Board-level story arc.
4. Slide-by-slide deck blueprint.
5. Decision slide.
6. Speaker notes.
7. Appendix plan.
8. Assumptions and source gaps.
9. Final board-readiness review.

Use answer-first slide titles, visual-first slide design, minimal text, quantified evidence, and explicit tradeoffs.
```

## Template B — Strategy deck

```text
Create a strategy deck that aligns senior leaders around a recommended direction.

Strategic question:
[QUESTION]

Audience:
[AUDIENCE]

Context:
[CONTEXT]

Evidence:
[SOURCES]

Expected decision or alignment:
[DECISION]

Use a situation-complication-question-answer storyline. Frame strategic alternatives, evaluate tradeoffs, recommend a path, quantify impact, and provide a practical implementation roadmap.
```

## Template C — Cybersecurity risk deck

```text
Create a board- and executive-ready cybersecurity risk deck.

Audience:
[AUDIENCE]

Decision or objective:
[OBJECTIVE]

Risk context:
[CONTEXT]

Security, risk, financial, and operational data:
[SOURCES]

Translate technical information into:
- Business resilience
- Customer trust
- Regulatory exposure
- Financial impact
- Service continuity
- Risk appetite
- Residual risk
- Investment tradeoffs
- Required executive decisions

Use risk heat maps, trend charts, control-coverage views, scenario analysis, investment bridges, and remediation roadmaps only where they add decision value.
```

## Template D — Existing deck redesign

```text
Critique and redesign the attached executive presentation.

Assess:
1. Storyline and governing thought.
2. Whether titles are answer-first.
3. Whether every slide has one message.
4. Text density and readability.
5. Data visualization quality.
6. Executive relevance.
7. Explicit decisions and recommendations.
8. Board-readiness.
9. Missing evidence or analysis.
10. Design consistency and visual impact.

Then produce:
- A revised deck architecture.
- Rewritten titles for every slide.
- A slide-by-slide redesign plan.
- Suggested visual replacements for bullet-heavy slides.
- A list of slides to delete, merge, move to appendix, or add.
- A final redesigned PowerPoint-ready slide blueprint.
```
